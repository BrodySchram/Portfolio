Assignment 1: Vega-Lite Multi-Plot Variation Study

Dataset Sourced: Montgomery County Commercial Liquor Sales Snapshot
Variables Tracked: whs_sales (Warehouse Sales), rtl_sales (Retail Sales), and item_type (Product Category).

1. Baseline Plot (Basic Scatter Plot)

Decision-Making Process: I initiated this study with a basic scatter plot to evaluate the baseline relationship between retail sales and warehouse sales volumes. By choosing standard, un-filtered linear axes, this chart clearly communicates how heavily skewed commercial inventory is, showing that a massive volume of entries cluster extremely close to low baseline sales volumes.

2. Variation A: Adding Color Categories

Decision-Making Process: For this first variation, I kept the identical linear spatial coordinates but introduced a brand new nominal dimension by mapping the 'Item Type' category to the color channel. This drastically shifts communication from a generic numerical correlation to a comparative product segment study, mapping out how product buckets (like Beer, Wine, or Spirits) differentiate in sales methods.

3. Variation B: Shifting to a Logarithmic Axis Scale

Description: Realizing that the tight bottom-left cluster in the previous charts obscured individual rows, I modified both the X and Y axes to use a logarithmic scale. This geometric stretching spreads out the heavily compressed low-tier data records, making dense overlapping rows remarkably easy to hover over and read visually without deleting any extreme sales outliers.

4. Variation C: Alternative Mark Type (Aggregated Bar Chart)

Decision-Making Process: In this final variation, I threw away individual coordinate plotting completely and switched the visual mark structure to an aggregated vertical bar chart. Instead of forcing viewers to evaluate hundreds of dots, this setup calculates the absolute average retail performance grouped cleanly by category. It trades granularity for a rapid, macroscopic performance breakdown.

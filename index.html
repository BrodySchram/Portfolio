  • This portfolio explores how a simple penguin dataset can be reframed through multiple chart designs. The goal is to show how changing the marks, scales, and grouping structure can reveal different patterns in the same biological measurements.

    What is a culmen? In penguin research, the culmen is the upper ridge of the bill—the lengthwise measurement from the base to the tip along the top of the beak. It is one of the standard measurements used to compare species and individual morphology.

    Chart 1: The Basic Plot (Standard Scatter Plot)

    Decision-Making Process & Communication: This plot establishes the baseline relationship between physical attributes across different penguin species, illustrating how bill length and depth vary together and how species clusters separate visually.

    Variation 1: Faceted Grid & Zoomable Axis Scale

    Decision-Making Process & Communication: This variation addresses overplotting by utilizing small-multiples structural faceting, moving each species into its own panel while preserving a shared scale structure for easier comparison across groups.

    Variation 2: Swapped Dimensions & Color Map Heatmap

    Decision-Making Process & Communication: One numeric axis is swapped out for the categorical species feature, while the displaced measure is encoded by color intensity. This emphasizes group-level differences and highlights how a categorical variable can structure the distribution.

    Variation 3: Binned Aggregation Matrix

    Decision-Making Process & Communication: This layout shifts focus away from individual coordinates to macro statistical aggregates. Binning transforms the continuous fields into density counts, allowing the viewer to see where observations concentrate across species and dimension pairings.
